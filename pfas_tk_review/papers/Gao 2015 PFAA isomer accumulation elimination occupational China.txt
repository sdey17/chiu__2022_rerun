  Differential Accumulation and Elimination Behavior of Perfluoroalkyl Acid Isomers in
  Occupational Workers in a Manufactory in China.
  Environ Sci Technol 2015;49(11):6953-6962. PMID 25927957. doi:10.1021/acs.est.5b00778.

ABSTRACT (verbatim)
"In this study, serum and urine samples were collected from 36 occupational workers in a
fluorochemical manufacturing plant in China from 2008 to 2012 to evaluate the body burden
and possible elimination of linear and branched perfluoroalkyl acids (PFAAs). Indoor dust,
total suspended particles (TSP), diet, and drinking water samples were also collected to
trace the occupational exposure pathway to PFAA isomers. The geometric mean concentrations
of perfluorooctanesulfonate (PFOS), perfluorooctanoate (PFOA), and perfluorohexanesulfonate
(PFHxS) isomers in the serum were 1386, 371, and 863 ng mL-1, respectively. The linear
isomer of PFOS, PFOA, and PFHxS was the most predominant PFAA in the serum, with mean
proportions of 63.3, 91.1, and 92.7% respectively, which were higher than the proportions
in urine. The most important exposure routes to PFAA isomers in the occupational workers
were considered to be the intake of indoor dust and TSP. A renal clearance estimation
indicated that branched PFAA isomers had a higher renal clearance rate than did the
corresponding linear isomers. Molecular docking modeling implied that linear PFOS (n-PFOS)
had a stronger interaction with human serum albumin (HSA) than branched isomers did, which
could decrease the proportion of n-PFOS in the blood of humans via the transport of HSA."

VALUES
  Serum GM (n=36 workers, Yingcheng Hubei plant, 2008-2012):
     PFOS 1386 ng/mL, PFOA 371 ng/mL, PFHxS 863 ng/mL
  Linear fraction in serum: PFOS 63.3%, PFOA 91.1%, PFHxS 92.7%  (all higher than in urine)
  Direction of the isomer effect on renal clearance: BRANCHED > LINEAR
  Proposed mechanism: stronger HSA binding of n-PFOS (molecular docking)

CONSISTENCY CHECK ACROSS THE ISOMER LITERATURE
  Gao 2015 (urine/serum, renal clearance):   branched cleared FASTER than linear.
  Kawabata 2025 (Chemosphere 385:144409, bile+urine+CSF): branched C7-C8 PFCAs had HIGHER
      renal AND higher total clearance than linear; C9+ no difference, because biliary/fecal
      clearance dominates from C9 and does not discriminate isomers.
  Xu 2020 Arvidsjaur (serum decay):          all three branched PFOS classes had SHORTER
      half-lives than L-PFOS (1.04-1.27 y vs 2.91 y).
  Li 2022 Ronneby (serum decay):             MIXED - 2/6m-PFOS 2.67 y and L-PFOS 2.73 y are
      indistinguishable, 3/4/5m-PFOS 3.43 y and 1m-PFOS 5.01 y are SLOWER than linear.
  Nilsson 2022b (serum decay, firefighters): branched FRACTION ROSE over 5 years, i.e.
      branched as a group cleared SLOWER; 1m-PFOS slowest at 11.5 y.
  Andersson 2025 (fecal+urinary mass balance, Ronneby): branched PFOS k 0.17-0.21/y vs
      L-PFOS 0.22/y, i.e. branched marginally SLOWER.
  => The sign of the isomer effect is NOT consistent across designs. The renal-clearance
     designs (Gao, Kawabata) say branched is faster; the serum-decay designs in
     sulfonate-dominated cohorts (Li 2022, Nilsson 2022b, Andersson 2025) say branched PFOS
     is the same or slower, with 1m-PFOS clearly slowest. A plausible reconciliation is that
     branched isomers are preferentially excreted by the KIDNEY but that the kidney is a
     minor route for PFOS (Andersson 2025: 85% of 2/6m-PFOS elimination is fecal), so a
     renal advantage does not translate into a shorter total half-life for sulfonates. For
     PFCAs, where urine is the major route, Kawabata's C7-C8 result and Xu 2020's
     carboxylate behaviour are consistent with that reading. This is an INFERENCE.
