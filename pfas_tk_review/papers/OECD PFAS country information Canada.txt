Overview of Risk Reduction Approaches

1. Class approach

On April 24, 2021, the Government of Canada published a Notice of intent to move forward with
activities to address the broad class of per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS). The class of PFAS
is based on the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) definition of
PFAS.

On May 20, 2023, the Government of Canada published the Draft State of PFAS Report and the Risk
Management Scope for PFAS for a 60-day public comment period. The comments received on these
documents were considered in developing the Updated Draft State of PFAS Report and Revised Risk
Management Scope.

On July 13, 2024, the Updated Draft State of PFAS Report, the Revised Risk Management Scope and
a response to comments document were published for a 60-day public consultation.

In March 2025, the State of PFAS Report was published. The proposed Order to list the class of PFAS,
excluding fluoropolymers as defined in the State of PFAS Report, to Part 2 of Schedule 1 to CEPA and
the Risk Management Approach for PFAS, Excluding Fluoropolymers were published for a 60-day
public comment period.

The latter outlines the phased approach to risk management for the class of PFAS, excluding
fluoropolymers, under regulation(s) made pursuant to the Canadian Environmental Protection Act,
1999 (CEPA):

   •   Phase 1: prohibition of the use of PFAS, not currently regulated, in firefighting foams,
       including C6 AFFF.
   •   Phase 2: prohibition of the uses of PFAS, not needed for the protection of health, safety or the
       environment, which includes consumer applications. Prioritisation of uses for prohibition is
       based on, and will take into account, costs and benefits, availability of feasible alternatives
       and other socio-economic considerations.
   •   Phase 3: prohibition of the uses of PFAS requiring further evaluation of the role of PFAS for
       which currently there may not be feasible alternatives and taking into consideration socio-
       economic factors.

Note: at each phase of risk management, exemptions will be considered, when necessary, with
attention to feasible alternatives, socio-economic factors and international alignment.

In September 2025, a Consultation Document on Phase 1 of the Risk Management of Per- and
Polyfluoroalkyl Substances (PFAS), Excluding Fluoropolymers: Prohibition of the Use of PFAS, not
Currently Regulated, in Firefighting Foams was published for a 60-day public comment period
ending on November 25, 2025. Comments received will be considered in the development of
proposed regulations.

2. Regulatory actions

Over the years, Canada has been implementing a combination of regulatory and voluntary actions to
reduce the risk of release and exposure to certain PFAS. Following scientific assessments in 2006
and 2012, three subgroups of PFAS (perfluorooctane sulfonate (PFOS), perfluorooctanoic acid
(PFOA) and long-chain perfluorocarboxylic acids (LC-PFCAs), their salts and their precursors) were
assessed as meeting the criteria under CEPA to be considered toxic to the environment. These
groups of substances were added to Schedule 1 to CEPA and accordingly were prohibited in Canada,
with limited exemptions, under the Prohibition of Certain Toxic Substances Regulations, 2012
(PCTSR).

On December 31, 2025, the Prohibition of Certain Toxic Substances Regulations, 2025 were
published in Canada Gazette, Part II. These regulations further restrict the manufacture, use, sale
and import of PFOS, PFOA and LC-PFCAs, their salts and precursors, and products containing them,
by removing or providing time limits for most remaining exemptions. These regulations repeal and
replace the Prohibition of Certain Toxic Substances Regulations, 2012 on June 30, 2026.

The New Substances Notification Regulations (Chemicals and Polymers) ensure that notified new
substances (not on the Domestic Substances List [DSL]) are assessed for potential risks to people in
Canada and the environment originating from industrial and other relevant uses. When assessing
PFAS, the New Substances program will consider the information included in the State of PFAS
Report, including the potential of combined exposure to multiple PFAS and the class conclusion. The
New Substances program will continue to consider updated information as appropriate.



3. Other measures

In addition, the Government of Canada published Federal Environmental Quality Guidelines for
PFOS for surface water for the protection of aquatic life, fish tissue, wildlife diet for mammalian and
avian consumers of aquatic biota, and in bird eggs, while the Canadian Council of Ministers of the
Environment (CCME) published Canadian Environmental Quality Guidelines for soil and
groundwater. The Objective for Canadian Drinking Water Quality for PFAS was published by Health
Canada in 2024. The objective of 30 ng/L applies to the sum of 25 specific PFAS.

With respect to federal contaminated sites, the Government of Canada continues to act through the
Federal Contaminated Sites Action Plan (FCSAP) to reduce environmental and human health risks
from these sites.

In 2006, Canada initiated the monitoring of PFAS in several media including air, water, sediment, fish
and wildlife, wastewater and biosolids. In addition, Canada has also undertaken biomonitoring of
certain PFAS as part of the Canadian Health Measures Survey since 2007.
