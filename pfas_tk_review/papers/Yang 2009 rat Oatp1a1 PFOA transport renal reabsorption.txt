2.7. Data analysis

    Non-speciﬁc passive diffusion of E3S or PFO to vector-transfected control cells
can be described by a non-saturable, linear kinetics (Han et al., 2008; Poirier et al.,
2008):

vpar = Pdif · [S]                                                                  (1)

where vpar is the velocity of E3S or PFO (pmol/min/mg) passive diffusion, [S] is the
substrate concentration (␮M), and Pdif is passive diffusion constant (␮l/min/mg) and
was obtained by linear regression to Eq. (1).
    The total cellular uptake of E3S or PFO in Oatp1a1-expressing CHO cells is con-
sidered to be the sum of the transporter-mediated uptake via Oatp1a1 (saturable)
and the non-speciﬁc passive diffusion (non-saturable):
         Vmax · [S]
vsum =              + Pdif [S]                                                     (2)
         Km + [S]
where vsum is the initial uptake velocity (pmol/min/mg) in Oatp1a1-expressing cells,
Vmax is the maximum velocity (pmol/min/mg) for Oatp1a1-mediated uptake, and
Km is the Michaelis constant (␮M) for Oatp1a1-mediated uptake. Vmax and Km were
obtained by non-linear regression to Eq. (2) with the input of Pdif from Eq. (1).
     When E3S concentration was much lower than the Km value of E3S uptake, the

                                                                                            4. Discussion

                                                                                                PFO elimination is highly species-dependent. Biological half-
                                                                                            lives of PFO range from hours for rabbits and female rats (Kemper,
                                                                                            2003; Kudo and Kawashima, 2003), days for male rats (Kemper,
                                                                                            2003), weeks for dogs, mice, and monkeys (Kudo and Kawashima,
                                                                                            2003; Butenhoff et al., 2004; Lou et al., 2009), to years for human
                                                                                            (Olsen et al., 2007). Among them, rats showed dramatic sex-related
                                                                                            difference, which is a rather convenient handle for the elucida-
                                                                                            tion of PFO elimination mechanism. The rat model could then be
                                                                                            used as a surrogate to bridge our understanding of PFO elimination
                                                                                            mechanism in other species, especially in humans.
                                                                                                In rats, PFO renal clearance was determined to be 23.35 and
