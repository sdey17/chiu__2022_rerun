## Q1. What are the measured serum/plasma elimination half-lives in rat and mouse, by chemical, sex, strain, dose and route?

### Takeaway
A dense measured record exists and it is consistent across laboratories: in the **rat**, PFOA, PFNA and PFHxS show 12–80× male-longer half-lives, PFHxA/PFBA/PFBS show 2–6× male-longer, and PFOS and PFDA show no male-longer difference (PFDA and one PFOS study show the *reverse*). In the **mouse**, PFOA, PFHxS and PFOS show essentially no sex difference, but **PFBA shows a 4.6–5.3× sex difference in the mouse too**, and PFBS a small (1.3×) one. The single largest caveat is that **no sex-resolved measured mouse PFOA half-life pair could be found anywhere**, so the headline 31× female mouse/rat ratio rests on a modelled value.

### Cited Findings

**Rat — PFOA (the extreme case)**
- Kemper 2003 (DuPont Haskell, tabulated by ATSDR 2021 Table 3-5), Sprague-Dawley, single oral gavage: males 202 h (SD 38) at 0.1 mg/kg, 138 h (32) at 1, 174 h (29) at 5, 157 h (38) at 25; females 3.2 h (0.9), 3.5 h (1.1), 4.6 h (0.6), 16.2 h (9.9) at the same doses. IV 1 mg/kg: male 185 h (19), female 2.8 h (0.5) — [ATSDR 2021 Toxicological Profile for Perfluoroalkyls, Table 3-5](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/toxprofiles/tp200.pdf)
- Same source, systemic clearance (Table 3-6): male 20.4–27.1 mL/day/kg across 0.1–25 mg/kg oral; female 655–1,164 mL/day/kg — a **~32× clearance difference** — [ATSDR 2021 Table 3-6](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/toxprofiles/tp200.pdf)
- Ohmori et al. 2003, Wistar, IV 20.1 mg/kg: **male 5.63 d vs female 0.08 d (70×)**; plasma protein binding >98% in both sexes; CL_tot–CL_R relation r²=0.981 — [Ohmori 2003, Toxicology 184:135](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12499116/), [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0300-483x(02)00573-5)
- Kudo et al. 2002, Wistar, IV 21.5 mg/kg: male 136 h (SD 24), female 1.9 h (0.7); CL 50.4 vs 2,233 mL/day/kg (**44× clearance**); the paper opens by stating the male t½ is "70 times longer than that in female rats" — [Kudo 2002, Chem Biol Interact 139:301](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11879818/); values tabulated in [ATSDR 2021 Tables 3-5/3-6](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/toxprofiles/tp200.pdf)
- Dzierlenga et al. 2020 (NTP), Hsd:Sprague-Dawley, gavage 6/12/48 mg/kg (M) and 40/80/320 mg/kg (F): **male 215 h vs female 2.75 h (78×)** — [Dzierlenga 2020, Xenobiotica 50:722](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31680603/), [doi](https://doi.org/10.1080/00498254.2019.1683776); dose design from [EPA 2024 PFOA assessment §3.3.2.1](https://www.epa.gov/sdwa/and-polyfluoroalkyl-substances-pfas)
- EPA notes the full measured rat range runs "from 0.03 days in females exposed to a high dose of 40 mg/kg (Dzierlenga et al. 2019a) to 13.4 days in males exposed to a relatively low dose of 0.4 mg/kg (Benskin et al. 2009)" — [EPA 2024 PFOA human health toxicity assessment, §3.3.1](https://www.epa.gov/sdwa/and-polyfluoroalkyl-substances-pfas)
- Kim et al. 2016, Sprague-Dawley: females 0.15–0.19 d vs males 1.6–1.8 d (**~10×**, the smallest ratio reported) — [Kim 2016, Food Chem Toxicol 97:243](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27637925/)
- Vanden Heuvel et al. 1991, Sprague-Dawley, [1-14C]PFOA 4 mg/kg IP: whole-body t½ **male 15 d vs female <1 d**; 24-h urinary recovery **6% (M) vs 91% (F)** — [Vanden Heuvel 1991, J Biochem Toxicol 6:83](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1941903/)
- Ylinen et al. 1990, Wistar, IP 50 mg/kg: male 105 h, female 24 h (only 4.4×, the outlier) — [ATSDR 2021 Table 3-5](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/toxprofiles/tp200.pdf); PubMed record has no abstract: [Ylinen 1990](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2306537/)
- Route matters: "Rats exposed by the IV route exhibited shorter half-lives than rats administered the same dose by the oral gavage route" — [EPA 2024 PFOA assessment §3.3.1](https://www.epa.gov/sdwa/and-polyfluoroalkyl-substances-pfas)

IV 4 mg/kg: male 13.0 d, female 0.70 d; IV β-phase male 33.8 d vs female 1.56 d. - Iwai 2011, ammonium perfluorohexanoate 50 mg/kg in mice and rats: >90% of dose recovered within 24 h "irrespective of gender or species", urine 73.0–90.2%, feces 7.0–15.5% — [Iwai 2011, Drug Chem Toxicol 34:341](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21696261/)

### Inferences
- Across the rat record the sex ratio is **chain-length graded**: C4 PFBA ~5–6×, C5 PFPeA ~1.75× (clearance), C6 PFHxA ~2–4.5×, C7 PFHpA ~2×, C8 PFOA 10–78×, C9 PFNA 12–22×, C10 PFDA ~1× or reversed. The PFOA/PFNA window is a genuine maximum, not a monotone trend.
- The mouse is **not** uniformly sex-indifferent. It shows a 4.6–5.3× PFBA sex difference and a 1.3× PFBS one, in the same male-longer direction as the rat, while showing essentially none for PFOA, PFHxS, PFOS and PFHxA. Whatever distinguishes the species is therefore specific to the **long-chain** compounds, not a blanket absence of sex-dependent renal handling in the mouse.
- Terminal half-life and clearance can dissociate (Olsen 2009 PFBS; Chengelis 2009 PFHxA IV vs oral). Half-life-only comparisons will under-report sex effects that are real in clearance terms.

### Gaps
- **No measured, sex-resolved mouse PFOA half-life pair was found in any source** — not in Lou 2009's abstract, not in EPA 2024, and ATSDR Table 3-5 has no PFOA–mouse rows at all. The female mouse/rat PFOA ratio of 31× therefore cannot be checked against measured data.
- Kemper 2003 and Kirkpatrick 2005 are unpublished reports readable only through ATSDR's tabulation.
- Argoul-Bernard et al. 2026 (eleven PFAS IV/oral in **female mice** by non-linear mixed-effects, [PMID 42162713](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42162713/)) would be the ideal modern mouse dataset; it was refused by the publisher.
- No strain comparison within mouse (CD-1 vs C57BL/6 vs BALB/c) for half-life was located. All the mouse PK studies retrieved used CD-1; Bogdanska 2014 used C57BL/6 but reported tissue distribution in males only ([PMID 24238775](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24238775/)).

## Q2. Why do female rats eliminate PFOA 20–40× faster than males? Which transporters, which are hormone-regulated, and what is the evidence?

### Takeaway
The difference is almost entirely **renal**, and the weight of evidence places it at the **apical (luminal) reabsorptive step** mediated by the androgen-driven, male-predominant **Oatp1a1 (Slco1a1)**, not at the basolateral secretory step. The hormonal evidence is unusually complete — castration, testosterone replacement, estradiol, ovariectomy, probenecid, hypophysectomy, GH-pattern replacement and ontogeny have all been done — but the specific transporter attribution rests on an in-vitro-to-in-vivo correlation rather than on a knockout.

### Cited Findings

**The difference is renal, and it is androgen-driven**
- Kudo 2002: the 70× t½ difference "is mainly due to the difference in renal clearance (CL_R), which was significantly reduced by probenecid, suggesting that PFOA is excreted by organic anion transporter(s). **Castration of male rats caused a 14-fold increase in the CL_R of PFOA**, which made it comparable with that of female rats. The elevated PFOA CL_R in castrated males was reduced by treating them with testosterone. Treatment of male rats with estradiol increased the CL_R of PFOA. In female rats, ovariectomy caused a significant increase in CL_R of PFOA, which was reduced by estradiol treatment. Treatments of female rats with testosterone reduced the CL_R" — [Kudo 2002, Chem Biol Interact 139:301](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11879818/)
- Vanden Heuvel 1992: castration greatly increased urinary PFOA elimination; castration + 17β-estradiol added nothing beyond castration; castration + physiological testosterone restored the intact-male phenotype. **In females neither ovariectomy nor ovariectomy + testosterone affected urinary PFOA elimination**, so "the inhibitory effect of testosterone on PFOA renal excretion is a male-specific response". Probenecid decreased renal excretion in castrated males but had **no effect in intact males** — the secretory pathway is only functionally present once androgen is withdrawn — [Vanden Heuvel 1992, J Biochem Toxicol 7:31](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1375295/)
- Ylinen 1989 (quantitative): intact females excreted 72±5% (n=6) of a 50 mg/kg IP dose in urine in 24 h vs 9±4% (n=6) in intact males. Castration + oestradiol (500 µg/kg every 2nd day × 14 d) raised male excretion to 68±14% (n=10); oestradiol alone in intact males gave 61±19% (n=10); castration alone helped but less. At 96 h serum PFOA in intact males was 17–40× higher than all other groups. **PFOA was bound similarly by male and female serum proteins and no phase II metabolism was detected in either sex** — [Ylinen 1989, Pharmacol Toxicol 65:165](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2587510/)
- Kudo 2001: castration abolished the sex difference in urinary PFOA; testosterone suppressed urinary PFOA in both castrated males and intact females, time- and dose-dependently — [Kudo 2001, Chem Biol Interact 134:203](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11311214/)
- Hanhijärvi 1987 (and the earlier Hanhijärvi 1982): "Active tubular **secretion** was observed only in the **female** kidney" — [Hanhijärvi 1987, Pharmacol Toxicol 61:66](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3628184/); [Hanhijärvi 1982, Proc Soc Exp Biol Med 171:50](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7145938/)
- Yadav 2023, pregnant SD rats, testosterone propionate GD15–20 (2-fold plasma testosterone rise), [14C]PFOA 9.4 pmol/kg IV on GD16: total elimination fell **12%**, tissue PFOA rose 12%, renal **Oat2 and Oat3 decreased** while **Oatp1 and Oat-K increased** — the predicted direction, but a small effect for an acute 2-fold hormone change — [Yadav 2023, J Environ Sci Public Health 7(3)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10470210/)

**Which transporters: direction and sex-dependence**
| Transporter | Gene | Membrane / role in rat | Rat sex bias | Hormonal control | PFOA substrate? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oat1 | Slc22a6 | basolateral, **secretion** | male > female | androgen-driven; **not** GH-driven | Yes, Km 51 µM (rat) |
| Oat3 | Slc22a8 | basolateral, **secretion** | male > female (kidney); ovariectomy raises it | androgen + GH | Yes, Km 80.2 µM (rat) |
| Oat2 | Slc22a7 | **apical** brush border S3 | **female >> male** (male = 13% of female mRNA) | strongly inhibited by testosterone; weakly raised by E2/P4; GH continuous infusion raises it | **No** |
| Oatp1a1 | Slco1a1 | **apical, reabsorption** | **male >> female** | increased by testosterone, decreased by estradiol | Yes, Km 126–162 µM |
| OAT-K | Slco1a3 | apical | male > female | androgen-driven | not directly tested |
| Urat1 | Slc22a12 | apical, reabsorption | — | — | **No strong interaction in rat** |
| Npt2 | Slc34a1 | apical | — | — | tested only by Katakura 2007, not retrieved |
| Mrp2/Mrp4 | Abcc2/Abcc4 | apical efflux | — | — | tested only by Katakura 2007 (Mrp2); not retrieved |

- Kudo 2002 measured renal mRNA for OAT1, OAT2, OAT3, oatp1, oatp2 and OAT-K under seven hormonal states: **OAT2 mRNA in males was only 13% of the female level**; castration or estradiol raised it, testosterone lowered it. **oatp1 and OAT-K were significantly higher in males** and were reduced by castration or estradiol. Ovariectomy raised OAT3. Multiple regression attributed the CL_R change "at least in part" to OAT2 and OAT3 — [Kudo 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11879818/)
- **But Nakagawa 2008 showed neither rat nor human OAT2 transports PFOA at all**, while rat Oat1 (Km 51.0±12.0 µM) and Oat3 (Km 80.2±17.8 µM) do — [Nakagawa 2008, Basic Clin Pharmacol Toxicol 103:1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18373647/)
- Weaver 2010 (rat transporters in CHO/HEK293): Oat1 Km 50.5 µM (C7) and 43.2 µM (C8); Oat3 Km 65.7 µM (C8) and 174.5 µM (C9); **Oatp1a1 Km 126.4 (C8), 20.5 (C9), 28.5 µM (C10)**. "Oat1 and Oat3 are involved in renal secretion of C7–C9, while Oatp1a1 can contribute to the reabsorption of C8 through C10, with highest affinities for C9 and C10." No strong inhibitors for Oat2 or Urat1 — [Weaver 2010, Toxicol Sci 113:305](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2807038/)
- Weaver 2010's own synthesis: "Since male rats have more Oatp1a1 present at the apical membrane, it is reasonable that in male rats less C8 and C9 are excreted in urine than in female rats" — [Weaver 2010 Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2807038/)
- Yang 2009: Oatp1a1-mediated PFOA uptake saturable with **Km 162.2±20.2 µM**, inhibited by sulfobromophthalein, taurocholate and naringin. log(Ki,app) was negatively linear in chain length and positively linear in log(total clearance in **male** rats) — "This in vitro-to-in vivo correlation strongly supports a tubular reabsorptive role of Oatp1a1… Due to the sex-dependent expression of Oatp1a1 in rat kidney, Oatp1a1-mediated tubular reabsorption is suggested to be the mechanism for the sex-dependent renal elimination of PFO in rats" — [Yang 2009, Toxicol Lett 190:163](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19616083/)
- Primary source for the male bias: Lu 1996 — male rat kidneys expressed at least six oatp transcripts; in females "renal oatp mRNA expression was markedly less, such that only the 3.2-kb band was consistently detectable"; **testosterone to females increased and estradiol to males decreased** renal oatp mRNA — [Lu 1996, Am J Physiol 270:F332](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8779895/)

**The decisive localisation experiments (non-PFAS substrates)**
- Gotoh 2002: urinary clearance of the Oatp1a1 substrate estradiol-17β-glucuronide was **>250× lower in male than female rats**, with no sex difference in biliary excretion or renal metabolism and with **plasma protein binding and inulin clearance equal between sexes** (excluding both binding and GFR). Male clearance relative to unbound plasma was <1% of GFR (proving reabsorption); dibromosulfophthalein co-infusion raised male clearance to the female level; **orchidectomy** markedly increased male excretion; **testosterone to females** reduced it to the male level — [Gotoh 2002, Am J Physiol Endocrinol Metab 282:E1245](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12006354/)
- Kato 2002: renal **uptake** clearance from the blood side showed no sex difference, but CL_urine,k of zenarestat was ~30× higher in females, and male CL_urine,p and CL_urine,k rose with dibromosulfophthalein — localising the sex difference to the **luminal** step — [Kato 2002, J Pharmacol Exp Ther 302:483](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12130705/)

**Quantitative reconstruction in a PBPK framework**
- Worley & Fisher 2015 built a rat PFOA PBPK model with explicit Oat1/Oat3 (basolateral secretion) and Oatp1a1 (apical reabsorption), using Km_apical = 52.3 µg/mL and Vmax_apical_invitro = 9,300 pmol/mg protein/min from Weaver 2010. Fitting to Kemper 2003 serum/urine gave **RAFapi = 35.0 in males vs 0.001356 in females**, a ~25,800-fold difference in fitted apical reabsorptive activity, with the basolateral term essentially unchanged between sexes. The model also reproduces saturation: at very high doses the predicted half-lives in both sexes converge on the transporter-off values — [Worley 2015, Toxicol Appl Pharmacol 289:428](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4662604/)
- >90% of PFOA is bound to serum albumin in rat blood, so only the free fraction is filtered — [Han 2003, Chem Res Toxicol 16:775](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12807361/), as parameterised in [Worley 2015 Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4662604/)

**Hypophysectomy, GH pattern and ontogeny**
- Buist & Klaassen 2003 (rat, gonadectomy + hypophysectomy + GH by injection vs infusion): rat Oat1 (kidney) and Oat3 (liver) are male-predominant, Oat2 (kidney) female-predominant. Renal Oat1 was lowered by gonadectomy and by hypophysectomy in males but **GH replacement did not restore it** (androgen-driven). Renal Oat2 was unaffected by gonadectomy but **dramatically decreased by hypophysectomy in females**, and **GH by continuous infusion (female pattern) raised Oat2 in both sexes** whereas injections did not — [Buist 2003, Drug Metab Dispos 31:559](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12695343/)
- Buist 2002 (rat ontogeny, day 0–45): at birth all kidney Oat mRNAs are low; renal Oat1 approaches adult levels at 30 days and **by days 40–45 Oat1 is greater in males** — the sex difference is post-pubertal — [Buist 2002, J Pharmacol Exp Ther 301:145](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11907168/)
- Ljubojević 2007 (protein level): rat renal OAT2 protein (~66 kDa) is female > male, localised **exclusively to the brush border of the S3 segment**, upregulated by castration, downregulated by ovariectomy, strongly downregulated by testosterone, weakly upregulated by estradiol and progesterone. **In prepubertal rats OAT2 protein was low and gender-independent** — [Ljubojević 2007, Am J Physiol Renal Physiol 292:F361](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16885152/)

### Inferences
- The field's consensus (Kudo 2002 → Lu 1996 → Weaver 2010 → Yang 2009 → Zhao 2017 → Worley 2015 → Niu 2023) is: **male rats reabsorb filtered PFOA from the tubular lumen via androgen-induced apical Oatp1a1 and therefore retain it; females, lacking that reabsorption, excrete essentially the filtered load.**
- The Gotoh 2002 >250× result establishes that Oatp1a1 alone can generate a sex difference in renal clearance *larger* than the 20–78× needed for PFOA. The mechanism is not quantitatively strained.
- **The Kudo 2002 regression on Oat2/Oat3 is directionally awkward and partly falsified.** Oat2 is the most spectacularly sex-divergent rat renal transporter (male = 13% of female), but Nakagawa 2008 showed it does not transport PFOA and Weaver 2010 found no chain-length-dependent interaction. Oat1 and Oat3 are *secretory* and *male*-predominant, which would make males eliminate PFOA faster. Only the apical reabsorptive arm has the right sign.
- The attribution to Oatp1a1 is therefore **correlative, not causal**: it rests on in vitro Km/Ki values, a male-dominant expression pattern, an IVIVE correlation and a PBPK fit — but on no knockout, no selective inhibitor in vivo, and no measurement of renal Oatp1a1 protein alongside PFOA clearance in the same animals.

### Gaps
- No Slco1a1-null rat or mouse has been dosed with PFOA. Zhao 2017 says explicitly of the analogous Oat1/Oat3 question that "experiments using the respective knockouts or selective inhibitors will be required to prove this hypothesis" — [Zhao 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6075085/)
- Vanden Heuvel 1992 and Kudo 2002 **disagree about the female rat**: Vanden Heuvel found neither ovariectomy nor ovariectomy + testosterone altered female urinary PFOA elimination, whereas Kudo found ovariectomy significantly increased and testosterone significantly decreased female CL_R. Unresolved.
- Katakura 2007, the only study to have tested **Npt2 (Slc34a1)** and **Mrp2 (Abcc2)** for PFOA, is not indexed in PubMed and could not be retrieved; no quantitative Npt2 or Mrp2 PFAS kinetics were obtained from any source. **MRP4 (Abcc4) does not appear to have been tested for PFAS at all** in the retrieved literature.
- Ljubojević 2004 (rat renal cortical Oat1/Oat3 castration/testosterone) was not retrieved; it is cited second-hand by Zurlinden 2025 and Zhao 2017.

## Q3. Why does the mouse NOT show this sex difference for PFOA? Is mouse Oatp1a1 sex-independent? What direct rat-vs-mouse transporter comparisons exist?

### Takeaway
**The simplest candidate explanation — that mouse renal Oatp1a1 is sex-independent — appears to be false.** Mouse renal Oatp1a1 is androgen-regulated and (per Cheng 2005 and Isern 2001) male-predominant, just as in the rat. The one unambiguous, *measured* rat-vs-mouse transporter species difference is **Oat2: strongly female-predominant in rat kidney, not female-predominant in mouse kidney (Buist & Klaassen 2004)** — but Oat2 does not transport PFOA, so it cannot be the answer either. The mouse's lack of a PFOA sex difference is, in the retrieved literature, **a documented phenomenon without an established mechanism**.

### Cited Findings

**The only direct rat-vs-mouse renal transporter comparison with PFAS relevance**
- Buist & Klaassen 2004, adult male and female 129J and C57BL/6 mice, branched-DNA mRNA, explicitly compared with rat: mouse Oat1 kidney-predominant and **male-predominant**; mouse Oat2 highest in kidney in both strains **without gender predominance**; mouse Oat3 highest in kidney with **liver** expression male-predominant, and **only 129J (not C57BL/6)** mice had higher renal Oat3 in females. Verbatim conclusion: "**The most notable species differences in Oat mRNA expression were a lack of Oat2 female predominance in mouse kidney and a less dramatic Oat3 male predominance in mouse liver.** With the exception of a significant species difference in Oat2 expression, many similarities were found between rat and mouse Oat mRNA levels." Ontogeny: Oat1 and Oat2 rise after day 25; Oat2 rises **30-fold** over 40 days vs ~2-fold for Oat1 and Oat3 — [Buist & Klaassen 2004, Drug Metab Dispos 32:620](https://doi.org/10.1124/dmd.32.6.620) (publisher `citation_abstract` metadata; body text 403)
- **Critically, Buist & Klaassen 2004 did not examine Oatp1a1/Slco1a1** — the transporter the PFOA field actually implicates. There is therefore **no direct rat-vs-mouse measurement of renal Oatp1a1** in the retrieved literature.

**Is mouse renal Oatp1a1 sex-independent? Evidence says no — but the mouse literature contradicts itself**
- Cheng 2005 (mouse, 15 Oatps, 12 tissues): "In kidney, expression of **Oatp1a1, 3a1, and 4c1 was higher in males than in females**"; in liver Oatp1a1 male-predominant, Oatp1a4/1a6 female-predominant — [Cheng 2005, Drug Metab Dispos 33:1062](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15843488/)
- Cheng 2006 (mouse, gonadectomy + hypophysectomy + *lit/lit* + GH/androgen replacement): "**Androgens increased Oatp1a1 mRNA in liver and kidney**, whereas male-pattern GH administration increased Oatp1a1 mRNA in livers but not in kidneys… In kidney, gender-divergent Oatp expression is **exclusively caused by stimulation by androgens**." Yet the same abstract's framing sentence states "in kidneys, Oatp1a1 and Oatp3a1 are both **female-predominant**" — **internally inconsistent with its own androgen result and with Cheng 2005** — [Cheng 2006, Mol Pharmacol 70:1291](https://doi.org/10.1124/mol.106.025122) (publisher `citation_abstract` metadata; body text 403)
- Isern 2001 (independent laboratory): "we describe the **strict androgen-dependent expression of mouse Oatp1 mRNA in kidney**"; mouse Oatp1 is the counterpart of rat Oatp1, Km ~8 µM for DHEA-sulfate and ~5 µM for estradiol-17-glucuronide. PFAS not tested — [Isern 2001, Biochim Biophys Acta 1518:73](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11267661/)
- Zurlinden et al. 2025, writing for EPA, treat the mouse transporter as androgen-regulated: they cite castration "reduc[ing] Oatp1a1 expression in the **male mouse** (Cheng et al. 2006) and male rat (Gotoh et al. 2002) kidneys. For each species, transporter expression is restored following supplementation with testosterone" — while simultaneously concluding that "this sex-dependent difference [in clearance] is not consistently observed across mice and non-human primates, indicating that there may be something unique about male vs. female rats" — [Zurlinden 2025, Toxicol Appl Pharmacol §4.3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12172007/)
- Ljubojević 2007 at the **protein** level found the opposite of Buist & Klaassen 2004 for Oat2: having shown rat renal OAT2 is female > male, androgen-inhibited and brush-border-localised, they report "**In mice, the expression pattern largely resembled that in rats**" — [Ljubojević 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16885152/)

**What the mouse does show**
- Kudo 2006: **hepatic concentrations of PFNA, PFOA and PFHpA were higher in male than in female mice**; PFHxA barely accumulated. No sex difference in peroxisomal β-oxidation induction; a single regression line related β-oxidation to hepatic PFCA concentration across C6–C9 — [Kudo 2006, Biol Pharm Bull 29:1952](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16946516/)
- Tatum-Gibbs 2011: "Hepatic uptake appears to be more efficient and storage capacity greater in **male mice** than in females" and "there is a significantly higher hepatic accumulation of PFNA in male mice than in females" — yet serum elimination was slightly *faster* in female mice — [Tatum-Gibbs 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21237237/)
- The mouse retains a sex difference in serum half-life for **PFBA (4.6–5.3×, Chang 2008)** and **PFBS (1.3×, Lau 2020)** — [Chang 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18353799/); [Lau 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9148184/)
- Sohlenius et al. 1992 (cited in Buist & Klaassen 2004's reference list): "The effects of perfluoro-octanoic acid on hepatic peroxisome proliferation and related parameters show **no sex-related differences in mice**" (Biochem J 285:779) — the classic early observation
- Mouse-specific efflux routes are established: Furukawa 2024 in knockout mice showed biliary PFOA was lower in Abcb4-null (0.25±0.12 µg/mL) than wild-type (0.87±0.02 µg/mL), and renal PFOA clearance was significantly **decreased** in Abcb1a/b-null mice — so in the mouse, PFOA elimination has substantial ABCB1-dependent renal and ABCB4-dependent biliary components — [Furukawa 2024, Environ Health Prev Med 29:21](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10965415/)

### Inferences
- The two explanations available in the literature for the mouse's lack of a PFOA sex difference are both **inadequate as stated**: (i) "mouse Oatp1a1 is sex-independent" is contradicted by Cheng 2005, Cheng 2006 and Isern 2001; (ii) "mouse Oat2 is not female-predominant" (Buist & Klaassen 2004) is the right *kind* of species difference but concerns a transporter Nakagawa 2008 showed does not carry PFOA.
- A plausible reconciliation not stated in any retrieved source: the mouse's quantitative **ratio** of apical reabsorptive capacity to basolateral secretory capacity, and the absolute abundance of renal Oatp1a1 relative to GFR, may be such that reabsorption is never rate-limiting in either sex — so an androgen-driven *fold-change* in Oatp1a1 produces little change in net clearance. Furukawa 2024's finding that ABCB1 contributes materially to mouse renal PFOA clearance, with substantial biliary ABCB4-dependent loss as well, is consistent with mouse PFOA elimination being distributed across several routes rather than gated by a single androgen-sensitive reabsorptive step. **This is an inference, not a finding.**
- The direction of the mouse sex difference in *liver* loading (male > female, Kudo 2006; Tatum-Gibbs 2011) is the same as in the rat, which suggests the hepatic arm of the sex difference is conserved while the renal arm is not.

### Gaps
- **The single most consequential missing experiment: no study has measured renal Oatp1a1/Slco1a1 protein in rat and mouse side by side, and none has dosed an Slco1a1-null mouse with PFAS.**
- The Cheng 2005 vs Cheng 2006 contradiction about the direction of mouse renal Oatp1a1 sex bias is unresolved and could not be adjudicated because both papers' body texts were refused (403 from ASPET/Elsevier).
- Buist & Klaassen 2004 vs Ljubojević 2007 disagree about whether mouse renal Oat2 shows female predominance (mRNA, no; protein, yes). Unresolved.
- Argoul-Bernard et al. 2026, "Comparative in vitro study of structure related uptake of ten PFAS by human and **rat** renal transporters" ([PMID 42446666](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42446666/)), is rat-vs-human, not rat-vs-mouse, and was not retrievable.

## Q4. Why does PFOS escape the sex effect in both species while PFOA does not? Sulfonate vs carboxylate, or chain length/binding?

### Takeaway
**It is not a simple sulfonate-vs-carboxylate substrate-specificity difference at the implicated transporter.** Rat Oatp1a1 transports PFOS with ~7× *higher* affinity than PFHxS (Km 37 vs 256 µM), and PFHxS does show a 15–18× rat sex difference while PFOS does not. The authors who measured this concluded explicitly that **"rOATP1A1 is not the major transporter for the renal elimination of PFOS"**. The better-supported explanation is that PFOS's renal handling is simply not rate-limiting for its overall clearance: with ~1,000× lower total clearance than PFOA, extra-renal retention (hepatic sequestration, enterohepatic recirculation, phospholipid/albumin binding) dominates, leaving no room for an androgen-sensitive reabsorptive step to express itself.

### Cited Findings
- Zhao 2017, rat OATP1A1 kinetics: **PFOS Km 37±19 µM, Vmax 0.66±0.14 nmol/mg protein/min, Vmax/Km 19 µL/mg/min; PFHxS Km 256±69 µM, Vmax 1.01±0.14, Vmax/Km 3.9**. Rat OATP1A5: PFBS Km 117±41, PFHxS 160±122, PFOS 55±18 µM — [Zhao 2017 Table 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6075085/)
- Zhao 2017's own reasoning: "Rat OATP1A1 is also expressed in the apical membrane of renal proximal tubular cells… In addition, rOATP1A1 is expressed in a male-dominant manner (Kato et al. 2002). We previously demonstrated that rOATP1A1 transports several PFCAs, such as PFOA, and hence, at least partly, contributes to the longer serum elimination half-life of PFOA in male rats… As for PFHxS, pharmacokinetic studies revealed that the elimination half-life is much slower in male rats (t½ = 29.1 days) than in female rats (t½ = 1.6 days)… Increased reabsorption of PFHxS in male rats via rOATP1A1 could be part of the mechanism… **Although rOATP1A1 can also transport PFOS, the elimination half-life of PFOS in male rats is somewhat shorter than in female rats (Chang et al. 2012), indicating that rOATP1A1 is not the major transporter for the renal elimination of PFOS**" — [Zhao 2017 Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6075085/)
- Huang 2019, all three sulfonates in one laboratory, one strain, one protocol: "**Sex differences in kinetics were found for PFBS and PFHxS but not PFOS**"; "The longest chain PFSA, PFOS, had a half-life in the range of 33–40 days and did not display a sex difference in half-life after IV or gavage administration" — [Huang 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6624215/)
- So within the sulfonate series, sex-dependence *decreases* with chain length (PFBS 2–6× → PFHxS 15–18× → PFOS ~1×) while within the carboxylate series it *peaks* at C8–C9. The two series do not behave as a clean functional-group dichotomy.
- Magnitude argument: rat PFOA clearance is 20–27 mL/day/kg (male) and 655–1,164 (female), whereas PFOS clearance is 7.33–11.3 (male) and 8.52–22.2 (female) — i.e. female PFOS clearance is **~50–100× lower than female PFOA clearance** — [ATSDR 2021 Table 3-6](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/toxprofiles/tp200.pdf)
- Extra-renal retention for PFOS is large and well documented: Huang 2019 found "Liver:plasma ratios of PFOS were the highest followed by PFHxS and PFBS" — [Huang 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6624215/); Johnson et al. 1984 showed cholestyramine enhances fecal 14C elimination after both [14C]PFOA and [14C]PFOS in rats, establishing **enterohepatic recirculation for both** — [Johnson 1984, Fundam Appl Toxicol 4:972](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6519377/)
- Human/rat hepatic uptake transporters handle PFOS avidly: all three human liver OATPs (1B1, 1B3, 2B1) transport PFBS, PFHxS and PFOS, with PFBS the least efficiently; rat OATP1A1, OATP1B2 and OATP2B1 transport all three PFSAs — [Zhao 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6075085/)
- Niu 2023 records the OATP2B1 apical uptake potency order for human kidney as **PFOS > PFHxS > PFNA > PFOA > PFBS**, i.e. PFOS is the *best* substrate of that reabsorptive route — [Niu 2023 Figure 2A](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10328216/)
- Binding is not the discriminator for the rat carboxylate sex difference: plasma protein binding was >98% for PFHpA, PFOA, PFNA and PFDA in both sexes and Vss was similar between PFCAs and between sexes — [Ohmori 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12499116/); PFOA was "similarly bound by the proteins in the serum of females and males" — [Ylinen 1989](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2587510/)
- For PFOS specifically, binding partners have now been tested in vivo: **FABP knockout did nothing** (Modaresi 2025) while **albumin knockout markedly altered PFOS toxicokinetics** (Kaye 2025) — [Modaresi 2025, Chem Res Toxicol 38:1576](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12442221/); [Kaye 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12422536/)

### Inferences
- The "sulfonate vs carboxylate" framing is **not supported** as a substrate-specificity explanation: PFOS is an excellent Oatp1a1 substrate. What distinguishes PFOS is that its *total* clearance is so low — dominated by liver sequestration and enterohepatic recycling — that a sex-dependent change in the renal reabsorptive step cannot move the whole-body half-life appreciably. In clearance-decomposition terms, the androgen-sensitive step has a small flux coefficient for PFOS and a large one for PFOA/PFNA/PFHxS.
- This also explains PFDA, the C10 carboxylate: Weaver 2010 found Oatp1a1 has its **highest** affinity for C10 (Km 28.5 µM), yet PFDA shows no sex difference or a reversed one. Both PFOS and PFDA are compounds whose renal route has become negligible — Kudo 2001 measured only **0.2% of a PFDA dose in urine in 120 h in male rats**, so there is almost nothing left for a reabsorptive transporter to modulate — [Weaver 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2807038/); [Kudo 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11311214/)
- The sex difference therefore requires a **window**: the compound must be eliminated substantially by the kidney (so renal handling matters), *and* must be a good enough Oatp1a1 ligand for the androgen-driven reabsorptive capacity to bite. C8–C9 carboxylates and C6 sulfonate fall in that window; C4–C7 are too poorly reabsorbed, C10 carboxylate and C8 sulfonate are too poorly excreted renally at all.

### Gaps
- Nobody has measured renal clearance of PFOS as a fraction of total clearance in male vs female rats with the explicit aim of testing this flux-coefficient argument. Kudo 2001 did exactly this for the carboxylates (urinary % of dose by chain length and sex) but not for the sulfonates.
- The two readings of Chang 2012's rat PFOS sex difference — ATSDR's tabulation (male longer) and Huang 2019's citation of it ("62 vs 38 days", female longer) — point in opposite directions and could not be reconciled because the primary paper was not retrievable.

## Q5. How does chain length modulate this?

### Takeaway
Two overlapping chain-length gradients act in opposite directions: **secretory** Oat1/Oat3 affinity peaks at C7–C9, and **reabsorptive** Oatp1a1 affinity rises monotonically from C6 to C10. Net urinary excretion falls steeply with chain length (92% → 55% → 2% → 0.2% of dose for C7 → C8 → C9 → C10 in male rats). Short-chain PFAS (C4–C5) are not Oatp1a1 ligands at all, which is why they are cleared in hours in both sexes and both species — though PFBA still retains a 5× sex difference in both rat and mouse, which the Oatp1a1 story does not explain.

### Cited Findings
- Kudo 2001, male Wistar rats, 120 h after IP injection, **% of dose in urine**: PFHpA (C7) **92%**, PFOA (C8) **55%**, PFNA (C9) **2.0%**, PFDA (C10) **0.2%**. In females: PFOA 80%, PFNA 51%. Feces <5% of dose for all four in both sexes. Biliary excretion rates were slower in males — [Kudo 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11311214/)
- Ohmori 2003, Wistar IV, t½ male/female (days): PFHpA 0.10/0.05, PFOA 5.63/0.08, PFNA 29.5/2.44, PFDA 39.9/58.6. "PFCAs having shorter carbon chain length showed higher CL_tot… There was a close relationship between CL_tot and CL_R (r²=0.981)" — [Ohmori 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12499116/)
- Weaver 2010 inhibition screen across C2–C18 at 10 µM: **Oat1** inhibited by C6, C7, C8 with **C7 strongest**; **Oat3** strongest for C8 and C9; **Oatp1a1** inhibited by C9, C10, C11 with **C10 strongest**. Km values: Oat1 C7 50.5, C8 43.2 µM; Oat3 C8 65.7, C9 174.5 µM; Oatp1a1 C8 126.4, C9 20.5, C10 28.5 µM — i.e. the reabsorptive optimum sits ~2 carbons longer than the secretory one — [Weaver 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2807038/)
- Yang 2009: **no apparent inhibition of Oatp1a1 by PFBA (C4) or PFPeA (C5) even at 1 mM**; inhibition appeared at C6 (1 mM) and increased monotonically to C10; log(Ki,app) was negatively linear in chain length and positively linear in log(male-rat total clearance) — [Yang 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19616083/)
- Huang 2019, sulfonate series, one strain and protocol: "In rats, the half-life of perfluoroalkyl sulfonic acids decreased with shorter chain lengths. Plasma half-life increased with longer chain length after gavage administration: PFBS - males averaged 3.3 h, females 1.3 h; PFHxS - males averaged 16.3 days, females 2.1 days; PFOS - males and females averaged ~20 days." AUC also increased with chain length — [Huang 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6624215/)
- Zhao 2017: among the human liver OATPs, **PFBS was transported with the lowest efficiency by all three** — chain-length dependence in the hepatic uptake arm too — [Zhao 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6075085/)
- Fujii 2015, C6–C14 in mice and humans: "short-chained PFCAs (C6 and C7) were rapidly eliminated in the urine, whereas long-chain PFCAs (C8 to C14) accumulated in the liver and were excreted slowly in feces. Urinary clearance of PFCAs in humans also decreased with increasing alkyl chain lengths, while **biliary clearances increased**. **C9 to C10 had the smallest total clearance for both mice and humans**" — [Fujii 2015, J Occup Health 57:1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25422127/)
- Russell 2013 for C6: half-lives in mice, rats, monkeys and humans "were proportional to body weight with no differences observed between genders, indicating similar volumes of distribution and similar elimination mechanisms among mammalian species" — the short-chain regime is where cross-species scaling actually works — [Russell 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24050716/)
- Kudo 2000 and Kudo 2006 show the toxicodynamic consequence: chain-length differences in peroxisomal β-oxidation induction potency (PFNA > PFOA > PFHpA > PFHxA) are fully explained by hepatic concentration, not intrinsic potency, in both rats and mice — [Kudo 2000, Chem Biol Interact 124:119](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10670823/); [Kudo 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16946516/)

### Inferences
- The rat sex ratio is maximal where **renal excretion is still substantial but Oatp1a1 reabsorption is already strong** — C8 and C9 carboxylates, C6 sulfonate. Below that window reabsorption is absent (C4–C5 are not Oatp1a1 ligands); above it renal excretion has essentially ceased (C10 carboxylate: 0.2% of dose in urine) and the compound is liver- and bile-dominated.
- **PFBA's ~5× sex difference in both rat and mouse is unexplained by the Oatp1a1 model**, since Yang 2009 showed C4 does not interact with Oatp1a1 at all. Some other sex-dependent process (a different short-chain transporter, or a sex difference in GFR-normalised handling, or Oat1-mediated secretion which *is* male-predominant in both species) must be operating for the C4 compounds. No retrieved study addresses this.

### Gaps
- No chain-length series has been run in the mouse in the way Kudo 2001 and Ohmori 2003 did it in the rat (same lab, same protocol, male and female, urinary % of dose). Fujii 2015 is the closest but followed mice for only 24 h, which EPA 2024 notes makes it unusable for the elimination phase.
- Km/Ki values for the *sulfonates* at rat Oatp1a1 exist only for PFHxS and PFOS (Zhao 2017); PFBS was not kinetically characterised at rOATP1A1.

## Q6. Intracellular binding proteins: α2u-globulin (which mice lack), L-FABP, organic-anion binding

### Takeaway
Both the male-rat-specific protein hypothesis and the FABP hypothesis have now been **tested and rejected** — α2u-globulin by direct affinity measurement (Kd ~10⁻³ M, far too weak) and L-FABP/I-FABP by double-knockout mouse toxicokinetics. **Albumin** is the binding protein that matters, and it does not differ between sexes.

### Cited Findings
- Han, Hinderliter, Snow & Jepson 2004 purified liver-form and kidney-form α2u-globulin from male rat urine and kidney. Both bind PFOA in vitro at the fatty-acid site, "**however, the binding affinities are relatively weak. The estimated dissociation constants are in the 10⁻³ M range, indicating that bindings of PFOA to either A2U_L or A2U_K cannot adequately explain the sex-dependent elimination of PFOA in rats, and it is unlikely that PFOA-A2U_K binding would induce A2U nephropathy**" — [Han 2004, Drug Chem Toxicol 27:341](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15573471/)
- Modaresi 2025, wild-type vs global L-FABP⁻/⁻ I-FABP⁻/⁻ mice, single oral PFOS at 0.1, 0.5 and 5 mg/kg with blood and excreta tracked for 65 days: "**FABP deletion did not significantly alter PFOS blood concentrations, tissue distribution, or elimination rates. Elimination half-lives, clearances, and volumes of distribution were consistent across genotypes, suggesting that neither L-FABP nor I-FABP are critical drivers for PFOS in vivo toxicokinetics.**" In vitro liver partition coefficients were similar between WT and knockout livers for **15 of 19 PFAS** — [Modaresi 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12442221/)
- Kaye 2025, albumin-null mice: "Toxicokinetics and perfluorooctanesulfonic acid-induced liver protein expression are **markedly altered** in mice lacking albumin" — [Kaye 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12422536/)
- >90% of PFOA is bound to rat serum albumin — [Han 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12807361/), as used in [Worley 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4662604/); >98% plasma binding for C7–C10 PFCAs in both sexes — [Ohmori 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12499116/); "PFOA was similarly bound by the proteins in the serum of females and males" — [Ylinen 1989](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2587510/)
- Gotoh 2002 independently excluded binding as the cause of a >250× renal clearance sex difference in the rat: "**Both plasma protein binding and inulin clearance were comparable in male and female rats**, suggesting that this gender difference cannot be explained by glomerular filtration" — [Gotoh 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12006354/)
- Niu 2023 keeps FABP and phospholipid binding on the agenda as the reason liver sequestration is large, noting that bile acids and phospholipids are endogenous FABP substrates structurally similar to PFCAs — [Niu 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10328216/)
- Ryu 2024 compared unbound fractions of PFAS across human and rodent tissues and asked whether rat liver is a suitable proxy for emerging PFAS — the relevant test of whether rat-mouse differences could be binding-driven — [Ryu 2024, Environ Sci Technol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11825104/)

### Inferences
- Since (i) α2u-globulin binds PFOA too weakly to matter, (ii) mice lack α2u-globulin yet show *larger* PFOA/PFNA hepatic sex differences than one would expect from a "no male-specific protein" model (Kudo 2006), and (iii) plasma binding is sex-identical in the rat, **the mouse's lack of α2u-globulin is a red herring for the rat-vs-mouse half-life difference**. This should be stated explicitly in the report, because the α2u hypothesis still circulates.
- The Modaresi 2025 and Kaye 2025 pair narrows the binding question to **albumin**, which is not sex-divergent — so binding cannot be the mechanism of either the rat sex difference or the rat-mouse species difference, and the explanation must be transport.

### Gaps
- No knockout test of FABP for a *carboxylate* (PFOA) has been done; Modaresi 2025 tested PFOS only.
- No measurement of rat vs mouse renal or hepatic albumin-binding affinity for PFOA was located.

## Q7. Enterohepatic recirculation and biliary excretion: does it differ between rat and mouse?

### Takeaway
Enterohepatic recirculation is established in the rat for both PFOA and PFOS and is molecularly characterised in the mouse (ABCB4 biliary efflux), but **no study has compared the two species' biliary or enterohepatic handling of the same PFAS under the same protocol**. Within the rat, biliary excretion is *slower in males*, which adds a second, same-signed contribution to the rat sex difference.

### Cited Findings
- Kudo 2001, rats: "Fecal elimination of PFCAs was not different between PFCAs in female rats and comparable to those in male rats" — fecal loss is <5% of dose for C7–C10 in both sexes — but "**The rates of biliary excretion of PFCAs in male rats were slower than those in female rats**" — [Kudo 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11311214/)
- Vanden Heuvel 1991 examined biliary PFOA in rats with renal pedicles ligated (to remove the urinary sex difference) and found 11% of infused PFOA extracted by the male liver in a single pass — [Vanden Heuvel 1991](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1941903/)
- Johnson et al. 1984: cholestyramine (a bile-acid sequestrant) enhanced fecal 14C elimination in rats after ammonium [14C]PFOA or potassium [14C]PFOS — direct evidence of **enterohepatic recirculation of both compounds in the rat** — [Johnson 1984](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6519377/)
- Zhao 2017: enterohepatic circulation of PFOS occurs "not only in humans, but also in rats (Johnson et al. 1984). So far the mechanism for intestinal reabsorption of PFOS, and also of PFBS and PFHxS, in rats could not be explained because, unlike in humans, **rASBT does not transport** [the PFSAs]" — a genuine rat-human species difference in the intestinal reabsorption step — [Zhao 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6075085/)
- Human intestinal reabsorption routes do exist: OATPs mediate PFOA uptake into Caco-2 cells, and hOATP2B1 is the first human intestinal transporter shown to mediate PFHxS reabsorption — [Kimura 2017, Toxicol Lett](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28552774/); [Kimura 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32964147/); [Zhao 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6075085/)
- Mouse: Furukawa 2024 in Abcb4-null mice found biliary PFOA 0.25±0.12 µg/mL vs 0.87±0.02 in wild-type, with ABCB4 localised to the hepatocyte apical membrane. Renal PFOA clearance was **higher** in Abcb4-null mice (compensatory: renal Abcb1 mRNA up, Abcb1b protein up in proximal tubules, Slc22a8/Oat3 unchanged), and in Abcb1a/b-null mice renal clearance was significantly **decreased**. Conclusion: "Abcb4 and Abcb1 are excretory transporters of PFOA into bile and urine, respectively" — [Furukawa 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10965415/)
- Furukawa 2024 also records the species framing: PFOA plasma t½ is "much shorter in mice (15–20 days), rats (<1–15 days), and cynomolgus monkeys (20–35 days) than in humans. The reason for the long half-life of PFOA in humans is because of the **virtual absence of renal excretion**, while biliary excretion of PFOA is high compared with renal excretion" — [Furukawa 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10965415/)
- Mouse hepatic uptake transporters are PFAS-regulated: PFOA decreased hepatic Oatp1a1, Oatp1a4 and Oatp1b2 mRNA and protein (not Ntcp); PFDA decreased all four and raised serum bile acids 300%, dose-dependently and PPARα-dependently — [Cheng & Klaassen 2008, Toxicol Sci 106:37](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2563139/). Hepatic Mrp2/3/4 are likewise Nrf2- and PPARα-regulated after PFOA and PFDA — [Maher 2008, Toxicol Sci 106:319](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2721675/)
- Across species for long chains, biliary clearance is the route that grows: "Urinary clearance of PFCAs in humans also decreased with increasing alkyl chain lengths, while **biliary clearances increased**" — [Fujii 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25422127/)

### Inferences
- The rat sex difference has **two same-signed components**: slower renal clearance *and* slower biliary excretion in males (Kudo 2001). Transporter accounts that focus only on the kidney are therefore incomplete, though the renal component dominates quantitatively (Ohmori 2003's r²=0.981 between CL_tot and CL_R).
- Mouse PFAS elimination is demonstrably **multi-route and compensating** (Furukawa 2024: knocking out the biliary route increases the renal route). A system with redundant, compensating elimination routes is intrinsically less sensitive to a change in any one of them — which is a plausible structural reason why androgen regulation of a single apical reabsorptive transporter produces a smaller whole-body effect in the mouse than in the rat. **This is an inference drawn from Furukawa 2024, not a finding stated there.**

### Gaps
- **No rat-vs-mouse comparison of biliary PFAS excretion under a common protocol was located.** Kudo 2001 cannulated rats; Furukawa 2024 used knockout mice; nothing bridges them.
- The rat intestinal reabsorption transporter for the sulfonates is unidentified (rASBT does not transport them, per Zhao 2017), and nothing comparable has been examined in the mouse.

## Q8. Strain differences

### Takeaway
Strain matters measurably in the transporter-expression literature (129J vs C57BL/6 mice differ in renal Oat3 sex bias) and probably accounts for part of the inter-study spread in rat half-lives, but **no study has deliberately compared PFAS half-lives across rat or mouse strains**. Essentially all mouse PK data are CD-1; rat data split between Sprague-Dawley (Charles River and Harlan/Hsd), Wistar and "CR".

### Cited Findings
- Buist & Klaassen 2004: "**only 129J mice had higher Oat3 mRNA levels in female kidney than in male kidney**" — i.e. the direction of a renal Oat sex bias is strain-dependent within the mouse — [Buist & Klaassen 2004](https://doi.org/10.1124/dmd.32.6.620)
- Rat strain × half-life, PFOA male: Wistar 5.63 d (Ohmori 2003) and 136 h = 5.7 d (Kudo 2002) vs Sprague-Dawley 138–202 h = 5.8–8.4 d oral (Kemper 2003), 215 h = 9.0 d (Dzierlenga 2020, Hsd:SD), 44 h = 1.8 d (Kim 2016, SD), 322 h = 13.4 d (Benskin 2009, SD) — the **within-SD spread (1.8–13.4 d) exceeds the Wistar-vs-SD difference**, so strain is not the dominant source of variance — [ATSDR Table 3-5](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/toxprofiles/tp200.pdf)
- Rat strain × PFOS: Huang 2019 used Hsd:Sprague-Dawley SD and found no sex difference; Chang 2012 used **Charles River** Sprague-Dawley and Huang 2019 reads it as showing a 2-fold female-longer difference, noting "One report did find a sex difference in half-life: **Charles River Sprague Dawley rats** followed for 10 weeks after an oral dose of 2 or 15 mg/kg showed a 2-fold increase in half-life in females compared to males, 62 vs 38 days" — the only place in the retrieved literature where a rat PFOS sex difference is attributed to a specific vendor strain — [Huang 2019 Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6624215/)
- Mouse strains used: CD-1 for every PK study retrieved (Chang 2008 PFBA, Chang 2012 PFOS, Sundström 2012 PFHxS, Tatum-Gibbs 2011 PFNA, Lau 2020 PFBS, Lou 2009 PFOA, Das 2008 PFBA); C57BL/6 only in Bogdanska 2014 (PFBS tissue distribution, males only) — [ATSDR Table 3-5](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/toxprofiles/tp200.pdf); [Bogdanska 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24238775/)
- EPA's PFOA PBPK work used CD-1 mouse data from Lou 2009 and C57BL/6 data from DeWitt 2008, modelling the two strains separately — acknowledging a strain effect without quantifying a half-life difference — [EPA 2024 PFOA assessment, Appendix](https://www.epa.gov/sdwa/and-polyfluoroalkyl-substances-pfas)
- Strain and sex interact for PFOA *effects* in mice: Rebholz 2016, "Hypercholesterolemia with consumption of PFOA-laced Western diets is dependent on **strain and sex** of mice" — [Rebholz 2016, Toxicol Rep 3:46](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4770828/); Tucker 2015 compared mammary effects in CD-1 and C57Bl/6 after perinatal PFOA — [Tucker 2015, Reprod Toxicol 54:26](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4464984/)

### Inferences
- Because the only mouse PFOA, PFHxS and PFNA PK datasets are **CD-1**, the EPA re-analysis's "mouse" values are really "CD-1 mouse" values, and the female mouse/rat ratios of 31× (PFOA), 15× (PFHxS) and 21× (PFNA) each rest on a **single CD-1 experiment**. The possibility that a C57BL/6 or BALB/c mouse would behave differently has not been tested.
- The Buist & Klaassen 2004 129J-vs-C57BL/6 Oat3 result is a warning that mouse renal transporter sex bias is not a species-level constant.

### Gaps
- No deliberate strain comparison of PFAS half-life in either species exists in the retrieved literature.
- No PFAS PK data in BALB/c mice or F344 rats were located.

## Q9. Dose levels and dose/concentration-dependent (saturable) elimination

### Takeaway
Saturable elimination is documented in both species and is the standard mechanistic reading of the dose-dependence: in the rat, clearance **rises** with dose (consistent with saturation of reabsorption); in the female mouse, clearance becomes non-linear above ~40 mg/kg single or ~5 mg/kg/d. Crucially, the doses used throughout are 10³–10⁵ times above human environmental serum levels, so transporter-mediated reabsorption is likely closer to saturation in the animal studies than at environmentally relevant doses — which biases cross-species extrapolation.

### Cited Findings
- EPA 2025 IRIS PFHxS: "the data of Huang et al. (2019a) indicate higher CL in male and female rats given a dose of 32 mg/kg compared with 4 and 16 mg/kg… it was **consistent with a mechanism of saturable renal resorption (Yang et al. 2009; Weaver et al. 2010)**… the CL estimated by Huang et al. (2019a) for 16 mg/kg in female rats was 25% higher than that estimated at 4 mg/kg and the CL for 16 mg/kg in male rats was 19% higher" — [EPA 2025 IRIS PFHxS, p. 3-22](https://iris.epa.gov/ChemicalLanding/&substance_nmbr=10006)
- Huang 2019 PFBS: "C_max and AUC increased with dose in male rats, but these increases did not appear to be dose-proportional. The dose-adjusted AUC decreased roughly 3-fold from the 4 to 100 mg/kg dose, while clearance increased 3-fold." For PFHxS the dose-adjusted AUC fell from 12.4 to 6.65 µM·h/(mg/kg) between 4 and 32 mg/kg in males. For PFOS the direction was **opposite**: "Clearance in animals dosed with 20 mg/kg was lower than clearance in 2 mg/kg animals" — [Huang 2019 Tables 2–4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6624215/)
- Huang 2019's own reading: "There was some evidence of saturation or induction of elimination with administration of these PFAS… **High-affinity resorption processes in the kidney may play a primary role in PFAS kinetics, leading to sex differences and longer half-lives for longer chain chemicals**, but also are [saturable]" — [Huang 2019 Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6624215/)
- Kemper 2003 PFOA in female rats: t½ rises from 3.2–4.6 h at 0.1–5 mg/kg to **16.2 h (SD 9.9) at 25 mg/kg** — a 3.5–5× lengthening with dose — [ATSDR Table 3-5](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/toxprofiles/tp200.pdf)
- Dzierlenga 2020 female rats were dosed at 40/80/320 mg/kg and EPA notes a half-life as short as 0.03 d at 40 mg/kg — [EPA 2024 PFOA assessment §3.3.1](https://www.epa.gov/sdwa/and-polyfluoroalkyl-substances-pfas)
- Mouse, Lou 2009: "For these model parameters **a single dose greater than 40 mg/kg, or a daily dose in excess of 5 mg/kg were necessary to observe nonlinear pharmacokinetics for PFOA in female CD1 mice**"; one- and two-compartment models could not reconcile the 1 and 10 mg/kg data with the 60 mg/kg female data or with repeated 20 mg/kg/d dosing, but the Andersen saturable-resorption model could. Fitted Tm = 860.9 (SE 1298.3) mg/L/h and KT = 0.0015 (0.0022) mg/L, with the authors noting the filtrate flow estimate "was too large to be consistent with a biological interpretation" — [Lou 2009, Toxicol Sci 107:331](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19005225/)
- Mouse, Tatum-Gibbs 2011 PFNA: "the rates of PFNA serum elimination are **non-linear with exposure dose**" in the mouse (34.3–68.9 d M, 25.8–68.4 d F across 1 and 10 mg/kg), whereas "Serum elimination of PFNA is by and large **linear** with exposure doses in the rat" — [Tatum-Gibbs 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21237237/)
- Mouse, Chang 2008 PFBA: male t½ 13.34 h at 10 mg/kg, 16.25 h at 30, then **5.22 h at 100** — the half-life falls at the top dose, and the sex ratio collapses from 4.6–5.3× to 1.9× — [Chang 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18353799/)
- Mouse, Lau 2020 PFBS: "The rate of PFBS clearance was **linear** with exposure doses" (30 and 300 mg/kg) — [Lau 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9148184/)
- Worley 2015: "Evaluation of serum half-life following simulation of administration of a wide range of doses reveals that the model is also capable of describing transporter saturation. In both the male and female rat models, comparison of predicted serum half-life at very high doses, when transporters would presumably be saturated, are very similar to predicted serum half-lives when model simulations are run with [transporters off]" — [Worley 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4662604/)
- Zurlinden 2025 on the dosimetry mismatch: "PFAS internal dosimetry in the animal tends to be much higher than typically seen for humans in the general population. For example, the lowest animal serum concentrations measured for PFOA in this study were on the order of 1–10 µg/L after 80 days following a 0.1 mg/kg dose in male rats (Kemper, 2003). Comparatively, serum concentrations of PFOA following environmentally relevant exposures in humans are on the order of 0.002–0.2 µg/L… **Under a saturable renal resorption hypothesis, fitting the one- and two-compartment PK models using data sets from the higher dose experiments would overestimate PFAS clearance at (lower) environmentally relevant doses**" — [Zurlinden 2025 Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12172007/)
- A second, non-saturation dose-related artefact: PFAS **downregulate their own transporters** at toxicological doses. PFOA decreased hepatic Oatp1a1/1a4/1b2 and PFDA decreased all four uptake transporters, PPARα-dependently — [Cheng & Klaassen 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2563139/); and PFOS increased URAT1 and GLUT9 abundance in human HK-2 cells — [Cui 2026, Ecotoxicol Environ Saf](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42801899/)
- A third: apparent oral bioavailability exceeds 100%. Huang 2019 reported F% of 133, 92 and 46 (male PFBS) and 166, 137, 97 (female PFBS), and 98/82/52 (male PFHxS) and 142/112/71 (female PFHxS). EPA calls this "discordant… suggestive that [route-independent distribution and elimination] is incorrect" and, because of it, **used only oral rat data** in the PFHxS assessment — [Huang 2019 Tables 2–3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6624215/); [EPA 2025 IRIS PFHxS, pp. 3-21 to 3-22](https://iris.epa.gov/ChemicalLanding/&substance_nmbr=10006)
- Zurlinden 2025 found the same route effect systematically: "IV routes of exposure generally produced larger clearances than gavage routes of exposure in the rat… a significant difference between IV and gavage clearance was determined for **female rats** when comparing the same dose within a study" for PFBA, PFBS, PFHxS, PFOA, PFOS and PFDA — [Zurlinden 2025 §3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12172007/)

### Inferences
- Saturable **reabsorption** predicts clearance rising with dose, which is what Huang 2019 and Kemper 2003 show for PFBS, PFHxS and (in the female rat half-life) PFOA. Saturable **binding/distribution** or transporter downregulation predicts the opposite, which is what PFOS shows in Huang 2019 and PFBA shows in the top-dose mouse. Both regimes are present in the dataset; "dose-dependent elimination" is not one phenomenon.
- Because the rat sex difference is itself produced by a saturable reabsorptive step, **the measured sex ratio should be dose-dependent and should shrink at high dose** — exactly what Kemper 2003's female PFOA data (3.2 h at 0.1 mg/kg → 16.2 h at 25 mg/kg, while male values barely move) and Chang 2008's mouse PFBA data show. Cross-study comparisons that pool doses will blur the ratio.
- The route effect (apparent F > 100%) is a serious confounder for any half-life comparison that mixes IV and gavage data, and it is specifically a **female rat** phenomenon in Zurlinden 2025's analysis — i.e. it attacks exactly the parameter the central puzzle depends on.

### Gaps
- No PFAS PK study in either species has been run at environmentally relevant serum concentrations, so the low-dose limit of the sex ratio is unmeasured in both.
- The mechanism of the >100% apparent oral bioavailability is unexplained; EPA offers only speculation about bound/free equilibration kinetics and gastric pH — [EPA 2025 IRIS PFHxS, p. 3-22](https://iris.epa.gov/ChemicalLanding/&substance_nmbr=10006)

## Q10. Where does the literature contradict the EPA re-analysis ratios given in the assignment? (explicit flags)

### Takeaway
The qualitative framing of the puzzle is correct and well supported — the rat-mouse discrepancy for PFOA, PFHxS and PFNA *is* produced by the female rat, and PFOS *does* escape in both species. But **four of the specific ratios are at odds with the measured literature**, and in the two most important cases the EPA fit *understates* the rat sex difference while resting on a single mouse study.

### Cited Findings — flagged discrepancies

**1. Rat PFOA female/male = 0.049 (20×) UNDERSTATES the measured difference, which is 70–78×.**
- Measured: Ohmori 2003 **70×** (5.63 d vs 0.08 d); Kudo 2002 states the male t½ is "**70 times** longer"; Dzierlenga 2020 **78×** (215 h vs 2.75 h). Kemper 2003 oral gives 63×, 39× and 38× at 0.1, 1 and 5 mg/kg and 9.7× at 25 mg/kg (the dose-dependence discussed in Q9). Only Kim 2016 (~10×) and Kemper's top dose are below 20×.
- Sources: [Ohmori 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12499116/); [Kudo 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11879818/); [Dzierlenga 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31680603/); [ATSDR Table 3-5](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/toxprofiles/tp200.pdf)
- Likely cause: the EPA value is a **composite t½ = ln2·Vd,ss/CL**, not a terminal β half-life, and the fitted female-rat Vd,ss of 0.649 L/kg is roughly twice the value for every other rat/PFAS combination in the same file (0.20–0.44 L/kg), which inflates the female half-life to 0.690 d (16.6 h). Measured female-rat PFOA terminal half-lives cluster at **1.9–4.6 h** across Kudo 2002, Ohmori 2003, Kemper 2003 and Kim 2016, with only Kemper's 25 mg/kg (16.2 h) and Ylinen 1990's IP 50 mg/kg (24 h) reaching the EPA value. Pooling the high-dose Dzierlenga female data (40–320 mg/kg) with low-dose data in a saturable system would do exactly this — [Zurlinden 2025 methods and Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12172007/); `species_dose/animal_pk.csv`

**2. Mouse PFNA male = 227 d is far outside the measured range of 34.3–68.9 d.**
- Tatum-Gibbs 2011, the **only** mouse PFNA dataset, reported "terminal estimated serum half-life of 25.8–68.4 days [females] and 34.3–68.9 days [males]". The EPA fit's 227 d (95% CrI 47–446) male mouse value exceeds the upper measured bound by 3.3×, and its credible interval is 10-fold wide — [Tatum-Gibbs 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21237237/); `animal_pk.csv`
- The EPA mouse PFNA F/M ratio of 0.26 also overstates what Tatum-Gibbs reported, which was that elimination is "**slightly** faster in females than males". A 3.8× sex difference is not "slightly".

**3. Mouse PFHxA female/male = 1.17 (female slightly slower) conflicts with the rat direction but agrees with the measured mouse literature; the rat PFHxA ratio of 0.36 is at the low end of the measured 2–4.5×.**
- Measured mouse: Gannon 2011 found Cmax "not appreciably different between male and female mice"; Russell 2013 found "**no differences observed between genders**" in mouse, rat, monkey or human PFHxA half-life. So the mouse value is defensible.
- But Russell 2013's claim of no rat sex difference conflicts with Chengelis 2009 (IV 2.4×, CL 6.7×), Gannon 2011 (2–3×) and Dzierlenga 2020 (4.5×). Russell 2013 is a cross-species re-analysis, the others are primary measurements — [Russell 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24050716/); [Chengelis 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19429410/); [Gannon 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21349313/); [Dzierlenga 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31680603/)

**4. PFBA mouse F/M = 0.26 and rat F/M = 0.28 are correct in direction and roughly correct in magnitude — and this is the finding that complicates the framing.** The assignment's framing implies the mouse "shows little or no sex difference", but for PFBA the mouse sex difference (4.6–5.3× measured, Chang 2008) is **as large as the rat's** (5.2–6.2× measured). The mouse-vs-rat contrast is specific to the long-chain compounds — [Chang 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18353799/)

**5. PFBS rat F/M = 0.34 sits between two directly conflicting primary studies.** Chengelis 2009 IV: 3.3× (M 2.1 h, F 0.64 h). Olsen 2009 IV: 1.14× (M 4.51 h, F 3.96 h), and oral actually reversed (M 4.68 h, F 7.42 h). Lau 2020 notes this explicitly: "The t½ estimates were substantially different between these two studies" — [Chengelis 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19429410/); [Olsen 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19059455/); [Lau 2020 Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9148184/). Huang 2019 (Hsd:SD, three doses) supports a ~2–3× difference, i.e. closer to Chengelis — [Huang 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6624215/)

**6. PFOS rat F/M = 0.96 is well supported, but one primary study dissents, and the dissent's direction is itself disputed.** Huang 2019, Kim 2016 and De Silva 2009 all show no sex difference. Chang 2012 does show one; ATSDR tabulates it as male-longer (1,495–1,707 h M vs 919–989 h F) while Huang 2019 reads the same paper as female-longer (62 vs 38 d). This internal inconsistency in the secondary literature could not be resolved without the primary paper — [Huang 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6624215/); [ATSDR Table 3-5](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/toxprofiles/tp200.pdf)

**7. The mouse/rat ratios for PFOA, PFHxS and PFNA each rest on exactly ONE mouse study.** Zurlinden 2025's Table 2 study-species matrix shows mouse data from: Chang 2012 (PFOS), Chang 2008 (PFBA), Daikin 2010 + Gannon 2011 (PFHxA), Lau 2020 (PFBS), **Lou 2009 (PFOA only)**, **Sundström 2012 (PFHxS only)**, **Tatum-Gibbs 2011 (PFNA only)** — [Zurlinden 2025 Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12172007/)

**8. The route confound attacks the female rat specifically.** Zurlinden 2025 found IV clearance significantly exceeded gavage clearance in **female rats** for PFBA, PFBS, PFHxS, PFOA, PFOS and PFDA, with apparent bioavailability >100%. EPA's PFHxS assessment responded by discarding all IV rat data. The Zurlinden fits that produce the ratios in the assignment pool IV and oral data — [Zurlinden 2025 §3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12172007/); [EPA 2025 IRIS PFHxS pp. 3-21/3-22](https://iris.epa.gov/ChemicalLanding/&substance_nmbr=10006)

### Inferences
- The central puzzle survives all of this intact and in fact gets **sharper**: the measured female-rat PFOA advantage is 70–78×, not 20×, so the female rat is even more exceptional than the EPA re-analysis suggests.
- The three largest mouse/rat ratios (PFOA 31×, PFNA 21×, PFHxS 15× in females) are each a **single-mouse-study estimate divided by a well-replicated rat estimate**. The rat side is solid; the mouse side is thin. Any report should say so.
- The PFBA result (a full ~5× sex difference in *both* species) is the most informative anomaly for mechanism, because it shows the mouse is capable of sex-dependent renal handling of a PFAS and therefore that the mouse's indifference for PFOA/PFHxS/PFOS is compound-specific rather than species-constitutional.

### Gaps
- Without the Lou 2009 full text, the sex-split mouse PFOA half-lives underlying the 31× ratio cannot be verified at all.
- Without Chang 2012's full text, the sign of the rat PFOS sex difference in that study cannot be settled.
