Overview of Risk Reduction Approaches for PFASs

In 2025 the Ministry of Climate and the Environment released its Action Plan for a Toxics Free Environment. The action plan points at PFAS as one of three top priorities in the work with
hazardous substances, and international concerted actions are indicated as the most efficient way to address the regulation of these substances. The Ministry calls for high international ambitions
for this group of very persistent and harmful chemicals.
PFAS are first and foremost addressed through EU legislation, including the chemicals regulation REACH, and at the global level, e.g. through the Stockholm Convention. Norway is taking an
active role in the regulatory development for PFAS and has contributed to several nominations and listings. Norway was a part of the group of five countries that proposed a broad restriction for
PFAS in all applications under REACH, often referred to as the universal PFAS restriction.
Long-time focus on the use of perfluorinated substances in ski wax from authorities and in media has resulted in the ban of use of such substances in ski lubrication for competitive skiers up to the
age of 16 by the Norwegian Ski Association in 2017. This resulted in a proposal to introduce such a ban internationally by The International Ski Federation (FIS) in all competitive ski disciplines – a
ban that was introduced and has applied since the 2023/2024 winter season.

Fluorine-containing fire-fighting foam is largely substituted with fluorine free-alternatives in Norwegian oil and gas industry and refineries, and the use and discharge of fluorine-containing foam has
decreased by about 99 %. Fluorine-containing fire-fighting foam has been substituted with fluorine-free alternatives in civil airports and at fire-fighting training sites with the military forces in
Norway.
In Norway, the polluter pays principle is strong, and the responsibility (including the costs) for remediation of PFAS-contaminated areas lies with the polluter.
The Norwegian national priority list is a government list of environmental contaminants posing serious threat to health and the environment, including around 81 substances or substance groups,
with the following PFAS PFHxA, HPFO-DA, PFBS, PFHxS, PFOS, PFOA and C9-C14 perfluorinated carboxylic acids. The national goal is to phase out the use and emissions of substances on
the priority list.
Remediation of contaminated sites has been performed or is currently ongoing at several military and civil airports. Additional remediation actions will be required in many years to come.
Remediation of contaminated sites at the Norwegian civil airports have been prioritized according to a risk assessment and cost/efficiency analyses.
PFAS-contamination from paper production industry has been detected in one of Norway's biggest lakes, Tyrifjorden. Remediation actions at the industry site are in progress.
Assessment and remediation of sites contaminated by historic PFAS uses are prioritized.
