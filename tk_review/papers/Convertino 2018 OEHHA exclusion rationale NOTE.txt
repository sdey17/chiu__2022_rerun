NOTE FILE assembled 2026-10-01. Answers the question "Convertino 2018 - OEHHA excluded it;
find out why and what it reported." Every quotation is from a document on disk in this
directory or from the PMC full text retrieved in this sweep.

=========================================================================================
1. WHAT CONVERTINO 2018 ACTUALLY IS AND WHAT IT REPORTED
=========================================================================================
CITATION: Convertino M, Church TR, Olsen GW, Liu Y, Doyle E, Elcombe CR, Barnett AL,
  Samuel LM, MacPherson IR, Evans TRJ. Stochastic Pharmacokinetic-Pharmacodynamic Modeling
  for Assessing the Systemic Health Risk of Perfluorooctanoate (PFOA).
  Toxicol Sci 2018;163(1):293-306. PMID 29462473. doi:10.1093/toxsci/kfy035. PMC5920327.
  Full text retrieved in this sweep:
  papers/Convertino 2018 PFOA clinical oncology infusion stochastic PKPD.txt

It is NOT an infusion study. From the abstract, verbatim:
  "A phase 1 dose-escalation trial assessed the chemotherapeutic potential of ammonium
   perfluorooctanoate (APFO). Forty-nine primarily solid-tumor cancer patients who failed
   standard therapy received weekly APFO doses (50-1200 mg) for 6 weeks. Clinical chemistries
   and plasma PFOA (anionic APFO) were measured predose and weekly thereafter."
Administration was ORAL (capsule). Verbatim from Methods:
  "Ammonium perfluorooctanoate was administered orally as a single dose of 50 mg in the
   morning after an overnight fast in the first cohort of 3 subjects..."
  "...subjects were requested to fast for 1 h after ingestion of APFO. If a subject vomited
   after ingestion of the oral capsule, then the time of the event was recorded but another
   capsule was not administered..."

WHAT IT REPORTED (verbatim from the abstract):
  "Based on the GEE, the average rates of change in total cholesterol and fT4 associated with
   increasing PFOA were approximately -1.2x10-3 mmol/l/uM and 2.8x10-3 pmol/l/uM,
   respectively. ... A decline in total cholesterol was observed, with a clear transition in
   shape and range of the pdfs, manifested by the maximum value of the Kullback-Leibler (KL)
   divergence, that occurred at plasma PFOA between 420 and 565 uM (175 000-230 000 ng/ml).
   High-density lipoprotein was unchanged. An increase in fT4 was observed at a higher PFOA
   transition point, albeit TSH was unchanged."
Other reported results: no DLT in more than one subject at any dose, so the protocol-defined
MTD was not reached; recommended phase 2 dose 1000 mg weekly; stable disease >12 weeks in 8
subjects; "ALT and all other liver enzymes appeared unaffected by plasma concentrations of
PFOA up to 1530 uM."

*** CRITICALLY: IT REPORTS NO HUMAN HALF-LIFE, NO CLEARANCE AND NO NUMERIC Vd. ***
The model has a volume term, but the fitted values are not tabulated in the paper and the
PK is explicitly deferred. Verbatim from Methods:
  "A complete PK assessment is reported elsewhere (Campbell et al., in preparation)."
And verbatim from the model description:
  "the parameters V (volume of distribution), ka, k12, k21, and k10 are calibrated
   simultaneously in a fitted model"
with the sensitivity analysis concluding, verbatim:
  "The volume V and the absorption rate ka did not seem to determine significantly the PK/PD
   dynamics."
So even within the paper, V is a poorly-identified nuisance parameter.

=========================================================================================
2. WHY OEHHA EXCLUDED IT - THE PRIMARY DOCUMENTATION
=========================================================================================
From papers/OEHHA 2024 PFOA PFOS PHG responses to comments.txt, Comment 2 and Response 2
(Section 4.2), verbatim:

  COMMENT 2: "In Section 4.2, 'Human data from the clinical trial reported in the Convertino
  study (2018) should not be considered. The people in the study were extremely ill with
  confounding underlying pathology that negates comparison to healthy people.
  Also note: Data from the same clinical trial reported in Convertino, 2018 were used in the
  human half-life estimate in Dourson, 2019 and appears to be included in the human PFOA
  half-life estimate in Table 4.7.1. Again, I argue that data/estimates from that study are
  highly confounded by underlying pathology and should NOT be used in estimating PFOA
  half-life in healthy people.'"

  RESPONSE 2: "OEHHA agrees that the human data from the clinical trial reported by
  Convertino et al. (2018) should not be used for PFOA half-life derivation. The PFOA
  half-life estimate adopted in this document did not rely on Convertino et al. (2018) data
  for a number of reasons, including the fact highlighted by this reviewer that the dataset
  was not derived from observations in healthy individuals."

OEHHA's model inventory table (papers/OEHHA 2024 PFOA PFOS PHG.txt, around line 18391)
characterises it, verbatim:
  "Convertino et al. (2018) | 2-cmtp | human | PFOA | [calibration data] Elcombe et al.
   (2013) | [evaluation data] Same as optimization data | Stochastic model for dose-response
   of adverse effects in a pharmacological trial of PFOA in human cancer patients"
i.e. OEHHA also records that the model was evaluated against the SAME data it was fitted to -
no out-of-sample validation.

=========================================================================================
3. SUMMARY OF THE GROUNDS FOR EXCLUSION
=========================================================================================
(a) Population: 49 patients with advanced solid tumours refractory to standard therapy,
    life expectancy >3 months. Hepatic, renal and nutritional status are not those of a
    healthy population, and albumin - the carrier that governs PFAS distribution - is
    routinely abnormal in advanced cancer. OEHHA accepted this as sufficient on its own.
(b) Dose and concentration range: plasma PFOA up to 1530 uM (~633,000 ng/mL), four to five
    orders of magnitude above environmental exposures, well into any plausible
    saturation of albumin binding and of renal/hepatic transport. A half-life measured there
    is not transferable to background exposure.
(c) The paper does not report a half-life at all; the PK was deferred to an unpublished
    manuscript (Campbell et al.), so any "Convertino half-life" in the secondary literature
    (e.g. as used in Dourson 2019) is a third-party derivation from the trial data, not a
    value published in Convertino 2018.
(d) The PK/PD model was evaluated on its own calibration data.
(e) Drug interactions were screened for (warfarin, phenytoin, tolbutamide excluded because
    APFO is a potent CYP2C inhibitor), which itself signals that the population was on
    multiple co-medications.

CONCLUSION FOR THIS REVIEW: Convertino 2018 is the highest-dose controlled human PFOA
exposure on record and the ONLY human study with measured plasma PFOA in the hundreds of
thousands of ng/mL. It is therefore uniquely informative about dose-dependence of
PFOA toxicodynamics, and uniquely uninformative about PFOA toxicokinetics in a general
population. It should appear in a dose-dependence table, not in a human half-life table.
The Campbell et al. PK manuscript (listed in this project as "Campbell/ARA 2022") is the
document that would actually carry the TK parameters.
