Overview of Risk Reduction Approaches for PFASs

In Australia, chemicals are regulated under national (Commonwealth), state and territory laws. At the Commonwealth level, chemicals are regulated according to their use. That is, industrial
chemicals, pesticides and veterinary medicines, therapeutic goods, and food, food additives and food packaging materials.
Australia is engaged in meeting its obligations under international agreements and initiatives on chemicals management. This includes the Stockholm and Rotterdam Conventions (controls on
certain high concern PFAS), Montreal Protocol (controls on ozone-depleting and synthetic greenhouse gas including certain PFAS), and the Basel and Waigani Conventions (includes controls on
PFAS-containing wastes).
Risk Assessment
The Australian Industrial Chemicals Introduction Scheme (AICIS) assesses risks to human health and the environment from the introduction (import or manufacture) and use of industrial
chemicals. These risk assessments are available publicly on the AICIS website.
When an AICIS risk assessment identifies risks that require risk management controls to protect human health or the environment, AICIS makes recommendations to the relevant risk
management agency. Risk management agencies regulate the use, release and disposal of industrial chemicals to protect human health and the environment within their respective risk
management frameworks. If the identified risks of the chemical cannot be managed within these frameworks, AICIS has the authority to refuse to authorise the introduction of an industrial
chemical, cancel an authorisation, remove the chemical from the Inventory, and/or vary the terms of Inventory listing or authorisation conditions.
Risk Management
The Industrial Chemicals Environmental Management Standard (IChEMS) was introduced to prevent harm to the environment by establishing nationally consistent standards for managing import,
export, manufacture, use and disposal of industrial chemicals. The IChEMS was developed collaboratively by the Commonwealth, states and territories, and all of these governments work
together to implement the scheme. Three groups of PFAS representing around 500 individual substances have been scheduled under the scheme: PFOS, PFOA, PFHxS and related chemicals. A
ban on the import, use and manufacture of these PFAS came into effect from 1 July 2025.
Contamination Management
The government works closely with states and territories to provide nationally consistent responses to PFAS contamination through the:
    -   Intergovernmental Agreement on a National Framework for Responding to PFAS;
    -   National PFAS Position Statement;
    -   PFAS National Environmental Management Plan; and
    -   Assessment of Site Contamination National Environment Protection Measure.
The Position Statement sets out the shared view of Australian governments that future PFAS use in Australia should be reduced to the greatest extent practicable. This includes working towards
the ultimate goal of transitioning away from the use of chemicals that cause irreversible or long-term contamination of Australia’s environment. The Position Statement lists a set of nationally
agreed objectives for phasing-out the use of PFASs of concern in Australia, including in articles.
The PFAS National Environmental Management Plan (PFAS NEMP 3.0) sets out nationally developed and agreed standards and guidance, which includes nationally agreed investigation
guideline values. The PFAS NEMP3.0 is the product of collaboration across the Commonwealth, states and territories and New Zealand. It recognises the need for sound regulation of PFAS in a
way that can be adapted to different state and territory regulatory requirements, local circumstances, and emerging priorities. The PFAS NEMP 3.0, released in March 2025, is implemented by
states and territories within their own environmental legislation.
