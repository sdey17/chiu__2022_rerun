    Abstract. Perfluoro-octanoic acid [PFO; CF 3(CF 2)6COOH] is a single chain
    fatty acid with surfactant properties. Thus far the kinetics and toxicity ofPFO
    has not been studied thoroughly.
        PFO was administered orally 3 mg/kg, 10 mg/kg, and 30 mg/kg to 36
    Wistar rats daily for 28 consecutive days. Another 12 animals received 0.5 ml/
    100 g saline each day. Animal behaviour, body weight, as well as, water and
    food consumption were observed regularly. Blood, urine, and some viscera
    were collected for the analysis ofPFO and histopathology.
        Food consumption decreased with increasing dose of PFO. Serum PFO
    concentrations were 6-19 times higher in the males than in the females (range
    48.6-83.0 J.lg/ml males, and 2.4-11.2 J.lg/ml females) (p<0.001).
        On the 7th day the mean PFO excretion in the low dose group for the males
    was 157±63 J.lg/24 (SD) hand 255±27 J.lg/24 h for females (p<0.05). In the
    high dose group the corresponding figures were 2476±665 J.lg/24 h (males)
    and 2917 ±493 J.lg/24 h (females). On the 28th day the PFO excretion for the
    low dose males was 609 ± 87 J.lg/24 h, 671 ± 65 J.lg/24 h for the females. In the
    high dose group the figures were 4619±2886 J.lg/24 h (males) and
    4379±692 J.lg/24 h (females).

Introduction

Perfluoro-octanoic acid [PFO, CF 3(CF 2)6COOH] is a single chain fatty acid de-
rivative. Perfluorinated fatty acids have commercial uses as corrosion inhibitors,
wetting agents, fire extinguishants, and as surface active agents (Olson and An-
dersen 1983). According to Griffith and Long (1980) the acute oral LDso for am-
monium-PFO was 680 mg/kg for the male, and 430 mgjkg for the female ChR-
CD albino rat. Still, a 90-day feeding study suggested, that the males were more
susceptible to the subchronic effects ofPFO than the female animals (Griffith and

Long 1980). The organic fluoride concentrations in the pooled serum were clearly
higher in the male than in the female rats. According to Hanhijarvi et al. (1982)
the female Holtzman rats had an active secretion mechanism for PFO in the kid-
ney. In the male rats the secretion was almost nonexistent.
    The aim of the present study was to study the effects of PFO in male and fe-
male Wi star rats during a 28-day oral toxicity study. Special attention was paid
to the analysis of the PFO concentrations in serum and urine in the course of the
study.

Materials and Methods

PFO was administered to 48 newly weaned Wistar rats in 0.9% NaCI solution
omg/kg, 3 mg/kg, 10 mg/kg, and 30 mg/kg by intragastric gavage for 28 consec-
utive days. The behaviour of the animals, and their feed and water consumption
were observed regularly. The rats were weighed once a week and urine was col-
lected on the 7th and 28th day of the study period. At the end of the study the
blood was collected with cardiac puncture whereafter the animals were necrop-
sied. Tissue specimens were fixed in 10% buffered formalin for histopathological
examination.
    PFO-concentrations in urine and serum were analyzed with a gas chromato-
graphical method described elsewhere (Ylinen et al. 1985). Statistical evaluation
was performed using SPSS statistical package installed in VAX 11/780 com-
puter.

Results

Two weeks from the start there appeared a dose-related decrease in the physical
activity of the animals. Lethargy, cyanosis, laboured ventilation and decreased
weight gain were observed. The female animals were affected earlier and more fre-
quently than the males. Food consumption decreased significantly with the in-
creasing dose ofPFO. The female animals receiving 30 mg/kg ofPFO showed the
decrease from the 10th day but all the dosed groups had reduzed food intake by
the third week.
    PFO concentrations in the plasma ranged between 48.6-83.0 Ilg/ml in the
males and 2.4-11.2 Ilg/ml in the female animals (p < 0.001). The smallest concen-
trations were observed in the group receiving 3 mg/kg and the highest in those ad-
ministered 10 mg/kg of PFO daily (Fig. 1). The differences between the dosed
groups within either sex were surprisingly small.

On both days (7th and 28th) the PFO-concentrations were the higher, the larger
the PFO dose. On the 28th day the PFO levels were 1.5-4 times higher than on
the 7th day of the study. Additionally, after the first week the females excreted
only a little more than the males, but at four weeks the difference had disappeared
or was reversed.
    The histopathological examination of the controls revealed one bronchopneu-
monia and another one with a small chronic inflammatory focus in the liver.
